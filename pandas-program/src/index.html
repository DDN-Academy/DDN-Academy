Your first three days

Forget the 16 weeks. Forget the calories. For the next three days you have one job only, and it is the smallest possible version of this whole program.

Day 1 — Eat breakfast

That’s it

Set the alarm for 6:00. Drink a glass of water. Then eat B6, the 90-second shake (page 12) — whey, milk, a banana, a handful of almonds. Ninety seconds. Do not attempt a cooked breakfast on day one; you’ll talk yourself out of it. Eat lunch and dinner however you normally would. Nothing else changes today.

Day 2 — Breakfast + one shop

20 minutes

Same breakfast. Then, on the way home, buy this and nothing else: eggs · a tub of Greek yogurt · six skyr pots · tinned tuna · tinned chickpeas · lemons · a bag of almonds · bananas. Twenty minutes and about €18. That single basket makes four different breakfasts, six snacks and two full lunches.

Day 3 — Add the mid-morning snack

10 seconds

Breakfast again. Then put a skyr pot and a handful of almonds in your bag the night before, and eat them at 10:00 between lectures. That’s the whole task. By the time you get home at 13:00 you will not be starving — and that is the first moment you’ll feel this working.

Then what?

On day 4, open the lunch pages and cook one. On day 7, start the dinner pages. Build it one meal at a time. Nobody changes five habits at once and nobody needs to — this took months to get into and it does not have to be undone in a weekend.

None of this is your fault

You’ve told me three things: you have no energy, you aren’t losing weight, and you’re losing muscle. They are not three problems. They are one — and it is the opposite of the one you’ve been blaming yourself for.

You are not eating too much. You are eating far too little, and almost no protein at all.

What your body did about it

When food stays scarce for months, a body doesn’t burn fat faster. It protects itself. It turns the thermostat down — less heat, less spontaneous movement, slower digestion, foggy head, cold hands, flat mood.

And it still needs amino acids every day to repair skin, hair, hormones and immune cells. If you aren’t eating any, it takes them from the only store you have: your muscle. That is the tissue you’ve been losing — and it was never a discipline problem.

Why the scale stopped

A body burning less needs less. Your maintenance drifted down until it met the tiny amount you were eating. That’s the stall — and it’s why eating even less has stopped working and never will again.

Muscle is also the tissue that burns calories at rest. Every kilo of it you lost made the problem harder. The way out isn’t less food. It’s more — and better built.

Three things to accept before you start

1
You’ll eat more than you have in a year.
About 1,900 kcal a day for the first three weeks. That is not a lot — it’s simply normal. It only feels enormous because you’ve been living on half of it.
2
The scale may go up in weeks 1–3.
By half a kilo to a kilo and a half. It is water, glycogen and food in your gut — the things that refill the moment you start eating. At these calories it is physically impossible for it to be fat. Do not panic, and do not cut.
3
Nothing here is “clean eating”.
There is bread, rice, olive oil, merguez, dates and dark chocolate in this book. You already cook better than most people ever will. The only things missing were quantity and protein.

The whole plan, in one sentence

Eat three real meals a day, put protein in every one, keep walking, go to Pilates, and sleep — and your body will finally let go of fat instead of muscle, because for the first time it can afford to.

Non-negotiables

Your seven promises

Not rules — promises to yourself. If you forget everything else in this book, keep these seven. They do ninety per cent of the work.

1

Three meals. Every day. No exceptions.

Even a small one, even late, even when you’re not hungry. Skipping is what taught your body to slow down; twenty-one meals a week is what reverses it. Tuna, chickpeas, olive oil and lemon in four minutes counts as a meal. Nothing does not.

2

Protein first, at every single meal

Build the plate around it, then add the rest. Breakfast 30 g Lunch 40–45 g Dinner 30 g Snacks 15–20 g It is the only macro that tells your body to keep and build muscle while losing fat — and it keeps you full for hours, which is why the 3 p.m. crash will disappear.

3

Never go below 1,600 kcal

Not on a busy day, not on a bad day, not to make up for yesterday. Below that line you’re back in the exact hole this book exists to get you out of. There is no version of this where eating less makes it faster.

4

Season everything properly

Harissa, garlic, cumin, sumac, lemon, olive oil, parsley and mint. Flavour costs almost no calories and it is the entire reason you’ll still be doing this in week twelve. Bland food isn’t healthier — it’s just food you’ll stop eating.

5

Two litres of water a day

Half a litre the moment you wake, before coffee. A bottle in your bag. A real part of the tiredness you feel at 11 a.m. is simply that you haven’t drunk anything since yesterday.

6

Lights out by 22:30

You wake at 6:00 — that’s 7.5 hours. Muscle is built and appetite hormones are reset while you sleep. Short nights raise hunger, drain willpower and waste the work you did in class.

7

Walk, every day, whatever else happens

Six to ten thousand steps. Walking burns more across a week than any workout would, costs you no recovery, and doesn’t make you ravenous the way cardio does. It happens on Pilates days too.

Turning Phase 1 into Phase 2 — change three things, nothing else

From week 4 you drop about 200 kcal a day. You do not drop a meal, and you don’t touch the protein or the olive oil. You shave the starch slightly, in three places:

Lunch grain

160 g cooked rice or bulgur → 100 g. About one serving spoon less. Fill the plate with more salad.

−70 kcal

Breakfast starch

Two slices of bread → one, or 45 g oats → 30 g. Same eggs, same yogurt, same nuts.

Why Phase 1 exists at all

Three weeks at maintenance before any deficit. It refills glycogen, restores your appetite signalling, brings your energy back and locks in the three-meal habit. Cutting from a starved baseline is how people lose muscle and stall by week five. In weeks 1–3, success is 21 days of three meals — not a number.

B1

Express Shakshuka ★

10 min
You need
  • 3 eggs
  • 200 g tinned crushed tomato (half a tin)
  • ½ onion or ½ green pepper, sliced thin
  • 1 tsp harissa · ½ tsp cumin · salt
  • 1 tsp olive oil · 1 small wholemeal pita (60 g)
Method

Oil in a small pan on high. Onion or pepper, 3 minutes. Tomato, harissa, cumin and salt, 3 minutes more until it thickens. Crack the eggs straight in, lid on, 4 minutes.

Eat it out of the pan with the pita. One pan to wash.

455kcal
27 gprotein
Sunday, cook the tomato base for four portions and keep it in a jar. That isn’t meal prep — it’s a sauce in the fridge. Breakfast then takes four minutes.

Lunch

L1

Chicken Shawarma Bowl ★

20 min
You need
  • 160 g chicken thigh or breast, in strips
  • Marinade: 2 tbsp yogurt · ½ lemon · 1 clove garlic · 1 tsp baharat · salt
  • 160 g cooked rice or bulgur (100 g in Phase 2)
  • Tomato, cucumber, red onion, parsley, sumac
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp tahini + water + lemon + garlic
Method

Marinate in the bowl you’ll eat from while the rice cooks — ten minutes is plenty, it doesn’t need an hour. Then: pan as hot as you dare, no crowding, 8 minutes, don’t stir constantly. You want dark edges.

Rice down, chicken on top, salad beside, tahini loosened with cold water over everything.

640kcal
46 gprotein
The one to learn first. Change only the spice and it becomes four different meals — see the box on page 16.
L2

Ojja Merguez

15 min
You need
  • 2 halal merguez (100 g), sliced thick — or 120 g halal chicken sausage
  • 1 green pepper · 2 tomatoes or ½ tin crushed
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp harissa · 1 tsp tabil or cumin · 2 cloves garlic
  • 60 g bread, for mopping
Method

Merguez in a dry pan first — it gives up its own fat and you cook everything else in it. Three minutes, then pepper and garlic, three more. Tomato, harissa and tabil in, and let it reduce properly — 5 minutes, until it stops being watery.

Two wells, two eggs, lid on, 3–4 minutes. Yolks still soft. Bread.

620kcal
38 gprotein
Merguez is fatty, which is why the portion is 100 g and why there’s no added oil. It’s in the budget — enjoy it once or twice a week.

Lablabi Bowl

Why dinner is the small meal

Not because eating at night makes you fat — it doesn’t. Because your appetite is largest at 13:00 when you get home, and because a lighter evening means you actually wake up hungry, which is what makes breakfast possible. Light is not the same as absent. Thirty grams of protein, every night.

Still hungry after dinner?

Drink 300 ml of water and wait ten minutes — thirst reads as hunger far more often than people believe. Still hungry? Then eat: a yogurt, some fruit, more salad, another egg. Real hunger after a 1,700 kcal day is information, not weakness — it usually means lunch was too small or you missed the afternoon snack. Fix that tomorrow rather than going to bed hungry tonight.

Six rules for the studio

  • Reformer over mat whenever you have the choice. Springs give you something to progress against; your own body weight doesn’t.
  • Ask for heavier springs. Tell the instructor your goal is strength, not “toning”. They’ll adjust for you — it’s a completely normal request.
  • The last 2–3 reps should be genuinely hard. Legs shaking isn’t a problem, it’s the point. A set that’s comfortable all the way through built nothing.
  • Progress the load, not the reps. Thirty easy repetitions is cardio. Ten hard ones is training.
  • Slow the lowering phase — three seconds down on everything. Free extra stimulus, no equipment needed.
  • Ankle weights or a band in mat classes if the studio allows. 1–2 kg changes everything on leg and glute work.

Never go to class fasted

Breakfast and the mid-morning snack are compulsory on class days. Training on an empty tank is exactly how you lose muscle instead of building it — the body has no fuel and no amino acids available, so it takes both from the tissue you’re trying to keep. If class is after 17:30, eat the afternoon snack first.

Protein around the class

Within roughly two hours either side — and the window really is that wide, so don’t stress. In practice: lunch at 13:15 before a 16:30 class, and yogurt or a shake straight after. The class is the signal to build; protein is the material. One without the other does nothing.

Supplements

You’ve never taken any, and you don’t need many. Two are genuinely useful here; a third depends on a blood test. Everything else in a supplement shop is a waste of money.

1 · Whey protein

The main tool

Why

Going from roughly 40 g of protein a day to 120 g is a big jump, and while your appetite is still small, food alone won’t get you there. One scoop is 24 g in thirty seconds — no cooking, no appetite required. It’s powdered milk protein. That’s all it is.

How

  • Dose: one scoop (~30 g) a day; two on a heavy day.
  • When: whichever meal is lightest — usually the mid-morning snack, or breakfast on a rushed morning, or right after Pilates.
  • Which: plain concentrate or isolate, vanilla or chocolate. Look for a halal mark and check for gelatin — HSN, Prozis and MyProtein all list halal-certified lines and deliver across Spain in a day or two.
  • Cost: €25–30 per kg — 33 servings, about €0.80 a day.
  • If it upsets your stomach: switch to isolate, far lower in lactose.

2 · Vitamin D3

1,000–2,000 IU daily

You leave at 07:20 and you’re indoors 08:00–12:00 — most of the useful daylight, especially between October and March. Low vitamin D is very common in students and presents as exactly what you’ve been describing: tired, flat, slow to recover, low mood. Take it with a meal containing fat; breakfast with eggs is ideal. Any farmacia sells it over the counter and a year’s supply costs under €15.

3 · Iron — only if a blood test says so

Do not self-prescribe

Low ferritin is the most common reason a twenty-year-old woman feels exhausted, and it’s very easy to fix — once it has been measured. Supplementing iron you don’t need is genuinely harmful, so this one needs a number, not a guess.

If the fatigue hasn’t lifted after four weeks of eating properly, ask a GP for ferritin, full blood count, vitamin D, B12 and TSH. Meanwhile the food does a lot of the work — L3, L8 and D2, always with lemon.

What to skip

Fat burners, detox teas, “slimming” infusions, CLA, L-carnitine, appetite suppressants, collagen for weight loss. None of them work, several aren’t halal-certified, and some sold online are genuinely unsafe. Creatine is the one other supplement with real evidence behind it, but it isn’t a priority for you now.

The 3 p.m. crash

It isn’t normal, it isn’t your personality, and it isn’t something to solve with more coffee. It’s a fuel problem caused by eating too little too late in the day.

It should disappear within about ten days of eating breakfast, a mid-morning snack and a real lunch — one of the first and clearest signs this is working.

Health notes

The morning-after pill you took four days ago

Tiredness, nausea, headache, sore breasts and light spotting are all common, well-documented side effects, and they usually settle within a few days to a couple of weeks — so some of the exhaustion you’re feeling right now has nothing to do with your diet. Two practical consequences:

  • Ignore the scale for the next ten to fourteen days. Hormonal water retention can easily add one to two kilos that have nothing whatsoever to do with fat. Weighing yourself in that window will only upset you over nothing.
  • Your next period may come early or late — up to about a week either way is expected. If it’s more than seven days later than you’d expect, take a pregnancy test: the pill reduces the chance of pregnancy but doesn’t eliminate it.

Start anyway. Eating properly will help the fatigue, not add to it.

Your cycle, and how to work with it

  • The week before your period: appetite up, water up, mood down. This is when plans get abandoned. Keep protein high, eat the carbs without guilt, and don’t weigh yourself. It passes in four days.
  • During your period: you lose iron. Prioritise L3, L8 and D2, always with lemon — vitamin C roughly doubles iron absorption. Keep going to Pilates unless you’re in real pain; gentle movement genuinely helps cramps.
  • If your periods are irregular, very light, or have stopped: that’s a sign your body has been under-fuelled for a long time. It’s a reason to eat more, not less — and a reason to see a doctor rather than wait it out.

See a doctor if — not optional

  • The fatigue hasn’t clearly improved after four weeks of eating three real meals a day.
  • Dizziness when you stand, hair falling out more than usual, feeling cold all the time, nails breaking, bruising easily.
  • Periods that stop for three months or more.
  • Heart racing at rest, or breathlessness on stairs you used to manage.
  • Food starts to feel like anxiety rather than fuel. Talk to someone early, not late — it’s far more common than people admit, and it is very treatable.

Week 16 is not the finish line

It’s where you stop losing and start maintaining — around 1,900 kcal, still 120 g of protein, still three classes a week. Almost everyone who regains weight does it because they treated the end of the diet as the end of the habits. The habits are the result; the weight is a side effect of them.

Read the weight column loosely

Those numbers are a trend line, not a schedule. Real weeks go: down 0.6, up 0.2, flat, down 0.5. Two flat weeks in a row is normal and means nothing. What matters is the direction across a month — and far more than the scale, what the tape around your waist is doing.

Three ways this goes wrong — all avoidable

Skipping Phase 1
Going straight into a deficit from a starved baseline. You lose muscle, your energy never recovers, and you stall by week five.
Cutting harder when it slows
The instinct that got you here. Below 1,600 kcal the body defends itself and the whole thing grinds to a halt.
Quitting in the flat month
There will be a stretch where the scale barely moves while your waist drops. That’s muscle replacing fat. Keep going.
Measure the right things

How to track progress

You’re going to build muscle while losing fat. That means there will be a month where the scale barely moves and your waist drops three centimetres. If the scale is the only thing you measure, you will quit during exactly that month. Let’s not let that happen.

Weekly · Saturday morning

Same day, same time, after the bathroom, before eating or drinking.

  1. Weight. Write it down — but only the four-week trend means anything. A single reading is noise.
  2. Waist, at the navel, tape flat, not pulled tight, not holding your breath in. This is the number that matters most.
  3. Hips, at the widest point.
  4. Photos every two weeks — front and side, same spot, same light. You won’t notice change day to day. You will absolutely notice it between photo one and photo six.

Daily · ten seconds in a notes app

Five ticks. That’s the whole system.

When it gets hard

Troubleshooting

Every one of these will happen at some point in sixteen weeks. Not one of them is a reason to stop.

“I’m never hungry in the morning.”

Completely expected, and temporary — your hunger signalling has gone quiet because you trained it to. Don’t force a full plate. Use B6, the 90-second shake, for the first five days; liquid asks nothing of your appetite. Then move to B3, then to the cooked ones. And stop drinking coffee before food — it’s suppressing what little appetite you have. Real morning hunger usually returns within two weeks.

“I don’t have time to cook.”

Nothing in this book takes more than 25 minutes and half of it takes under ten. D5 (5 min), D8 (4 min), L6 (10 min) and B3 (2 min) involve no cooking at all. On the very worst day: tuna, chickpeas, olive oil, lemon, parsley, bread. Four minutes, 40 g of protein — a complete meal, not a compromise.

“I skipped a meal.”

Then eat the next one normally. That is the entire answer. Do not eat less later to balance it — that’s the exact loop that created this problem. A missed meal is a missed meal, not a debt to be repaid.

“I ate way too much / there was a big family meal.”

Good. Enjoy it fully and go back to normal at the very next meal — see Day C on page 21. One large meal cannot undo a week; it isn’t mathematically possible. Under-eating for three days afterwards to “fix” it absolutely can, because that’s three days of no protein, no energy and a signal to your body that food is scarce again.

“The scale went up.”

Run the list: more salt, more carbs, period due, trained yesterday, constipated, drank less water — or, most likely in month one, you’ve just started eating properly and your muscles are refilling with glycogen and water. Every one of those adds weight that is not fat. Weigh again in three days and look at the trend, not the reading.

“I’m bored of the food.”

Rotate the spice, not the recipe — page 16. The same 150 g of chicken becomes shawarma, Tunisian, musakhan or tagine depending on one jar. Three minutes of thought, no new shopping, an entirely different meal. And swap the grain: rice, bulgur, freekeh, couscous and potatoes are interchangeable.

“Someone cooked for me and I can’t control the portions.”

Eat it, and enjoy it. Take a normal plate, go to the protein first, take one round of bread rather than three, and don’t take a second helping automatically. Tunisian and Syrian home cooking was never the problem — the problem was eating almost nothing at all. Refusing food at your family’s table to protect a calorie target is the wrong trade, every time.

“Pilates was cancelled this week.”

Change nothing about the food. Add 2,000 steps that day and carry on. One missed class across sixteen weeks is noise. Two missed weeks in a row is worth solving — find a second studio or a different time slot so a cancellation never costs you the whole week.

“I’ve been stuck at the same weight for three weeks.”

First, check honestly with your daily log in front of you: are you actually eating three meals? Actually hitting 120 g of protein? Actually getting the steps? Nine plateaus out of ten are one of those three, and the fix is to do them — not to eat less.

If all three are genuinely yes for three straight weeks and the waist hasn’t moved either, reduce by about 100 kcal — one less tablespoon of oil, or a slightly smaller grain portion at lunch. That’s it. Never below 1,600.

“It’s the week before my period and I want to eat everything.”

Because your body genuinely needs a little more then — physiology, not a failure of discipline. Keep the protein high, let yourself have the extra carbs, have the dark chocolate, and don’t weigh yourself. It lasts about four days. Fighting it costs more than it saves.

“I feel guilty when I eat.”

Read this slowly: at 59 kg and 1.60 m, eating 1,700–1,900 kcal a day, you are not overeating. You are eating what a healthy adult woman needs. The guilt is a habit your body learned during a long stretch of under-eating, and it fades as regular eating becomes normal again. If it doesn’t fade — or it gets louder — talk to someone about it. That’s a real thing, it’s common, and it’s treatable.

“I’ve fallen off completely. It’s been a week.”

Then you start again at the next meal. Not Monday, not next month — the next meal. Nothing is lost: a week off doesn’t undo six weeks on, and the habits are still in your hands. Go back to page 4 and do the three-day version again. Everyone who finishes something like this has fallen off at least once. The only difference between them and everyone else is that they restarted quickly instead of quietly deciding they’d failed.

Madrid, without the maths

Eating out

You are twenty and you live in Madrid. You’re going to eat out, and that is entirely fine — it just needs a default order so you don’t have to think about it every time.

Shawarma & kebab

Lavapiés, Tetuán — and halal by default

Ask for a plato rather than a durum: meat, salad and a little rice instead of a huge wrap. Skip the chips and the fizzy drink. Extra garlic sauce is fine. Easily 45 g of protein. The best fast food in the city for you.

Poke bowls & salad bars

Poke House, Honest Greens

Double the protein — it’s usually €2–3 and it doubles the point of the meal. Half rice, half greens. Avocado yes; creamy sauce and crispy onions and mango together, no. Pick one indulgence, not three.

A Spanish menú del día

Starter: salad, gazpacho or vegetable soup. Main: anything a la plancha — pollo, merluza, atún, ternera. Dessert: fruit or yogurt. Avoid jamón, chorizo, morcilla, panceta and bacon, which rules out most of the bocadillo menu anyway.

Breakfast or coffee out

Tostada con tomate plus a portion of tortilla española and a café con leche is a genuinely decent 25 g breakfast. Ask for an extra egg if they’ll do it. Avoid the pastry case — a napolitana is 400 kcal and 5 g of protein.

Supermarket lunch in a hurry

Rotisserie chicken (pollo asado, check it’s halal, or tinned tuna instead) + a bag of salad + a pot of hummus + fruit. About €6, 40 g of protein, no cooking, eaten on a bench. Better than most restaurant meals and a quarter of the price.

Coffee shops

Café con leche, not a frappuccino. A large flavoured or iced coffee can carry 350–450 kcal, almost all sugar, with two grams of protein — a meal’s worth of calories that leaves you hungry an hour later.

The only rule that matters when eating out

Find the protein, and don’t skip the meals around it. If lunch out was big, make dinner one of the light no-cook plates — D5 or D8. If lunch out was small and mostly bread, add a yogurt or a shake when you get home. Eating out is a normal part of a normal life; it only causes problems when it becomes a reason to skip everything else that day.

Panda — you are not lazy and your body is not broken.
