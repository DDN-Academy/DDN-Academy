None of this is your fault

No energy. No weight coming off. Losing muscle. Those aren’t three problems — they’re one, and it’s the opposite of what you’ve been blaming yourself for.

You are not eating too much. You’re eating far too little, and almost no protein.

What your body did

Months of too little food and a body doesn’t burn fat faster — it protects itself. It turns the thermostat down: less heat, less movement, slower digestion, foggy head, cold hands, flat mood.

And it still needs protein every day for skin, hair, hormones and immune cells. If you don’t eat any, it takes it from the only store you have — your muscle.

Why the scale stopped

A body burning less needs less. Your maintenance dropped until it met the tiny amount you were eating. That’s the stall — and it’s why eating even less has stopped working and never will again.

Muscle is also what burns calories at rest. Every kilo you lost made it harder. The way out isn’t less food. It’s more, and better built.

Three things to know before you start

1
You’ll eat more than you have in a year.
1,900 kcal for two weeks, then 1,600. That is not a lot — it’s normal. It only feels enormous because you’ve been living on half of it.
2
The scale may go up in the first two weeks.
Half a kilo to a kilo. It’s water, glycogen and food in your gut refilling. At these calories it is physically impossible for it to be fat. Don’t panic, don’t cut.
3
Nothing is forbidden.
Bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, olive oil, merguez, dates, dark chocolate — all in the plan. The only things missing from how you eat were quantity and protein.

The whole plan, in one sentence

Eat three real meals, put protein in every one, walk, and train your legs hard three times a week — and your body will finally let go of fat instead of muscle, because for the first time it can afford to.

The honest answer about speed

Two months or four?

Both get you to roughly 55 kg. They do not give you the same body — and since the goal is fat off and glutes on, the difference is enormous.

Never train fasted

Breakfast is compulsory on class days, and take the protein drink at 10:00 rather than the fruit. Training on empty is exactly how you lose muscle instead of building it — the body has no fuel and no amino acids, so it takes both from your legs. Protein within two hours after.

What not to add

No running, no HIIT, no extra cardio. It eats into the recovery your legs need to grow, raises your appetite sharply, and adds stress to a body that’s been under-fuelled for a long time. The fat comes off through food. The shape comes from Pilates and walking.

Class cancelled? Nothing about the food changes. Add 2,000 steps that day and carry on. One missed class in sixteen weeks is noise. Two missed weeks in a row is worth solving — find a second studio or a different time slot so a cancellation never costs you the week.
The part that decides the shape

Building glutes & legs with Pilates

Pilates absolutely can build muscle — but only when it’s treated as resistance training rather than a stretching class. The whole difference is in how hard the last three repetitions are.

Say this to your instructor

“My goal is building strength in my legs and glutes, not toning. Can you give me heavier springs and fewer reps?”

It’s a completely normal request and any decent instructor will adapt the class for you. Reformer over mat, every time — springs give you something to progress against; your own body weight doesn’t.

The five rules that make it work

  • 8–12 reps, not 30. Thirty easy reps is cardio. Ten hard ones is training.
  • The last 2–3 reps must be genuinely hard. Shaking is the point, not a problem.
  • Three seconds on the lowering phase of everything. Free extra stimulus.
  • Add spring before you add reps. Load is what builds; reps just make it longer.
  • Write down your springs. If they haven’t gone up in a month, nothing is being built.

The exercises to ask for — these are the glute and leg builders

  • Reformer footwork, heavy springs — heels, toes, wide. Your main quad and glute lift.
  • Single-leg footwork / leg press — twice the load per leg, and it exposes the weaker side.
  • Pelvic curl & bridging on the reformer — the most direct glute exercise in the repertoire.
  • Feet-in-straps series — hip extension, circles, frogs. Heavy spring, slow.
  • Side-lying leg series against a spring — glute medius, the muscle that gives the shape from behind.
  • Standing lunges & split squats on the reformer — ask for these specifically.
  • Step-ups and hip work on the chair — if the studio has a Wunda chair, use it.
  • Jump board — if available, at the end, for power.

What to expect, and when

Weeks 1–4
Sore in new places. Classes feel harder — because you finally have the energy to work properly.

One honest thing

Three reformer classes a week, run the way this page describes, will build real glute and leg muscle — especially in your first year of training and while eating properly for the first time in ages. It is not the fastest possible route: two extra 20-minute sessions of loaded leg work each week would roughly double the rate. You’ve said that’s not happening, and that’s completely fine — you just deserve to know that the springs, the protein and the hills are now doing all of the work, so none of the three can be treated as optional.

Two supplements · four habits · one honest page

The rest of it

Whey protein

1–2 scoops a day

Going from 40 g of protein a day to 130 g is a big jump, and food alone won’t get you there while your appetite is still small. One scoop is 24 g in thirty seconds — it’s powdered milk protein, that’s all.

Half a scoop in breakfast, every day, plus one in the 10:00 shaker on Pilates days. Plain concentrate or isolate, halal mark, no gelatin — HSN, Prozis and MyProtein all deliver across Spain in a day or two. About €0.80 a day.

Vitamin D3

1,000–2,000 IU

You leave at 07:20 and you’re indoors 08:00–12:00 — most of the useful daylight, especially October to March. Low vitamin D is common in students and shows up as exactly what you’ve described: tired and flat.

Take it with breakfast; any farmacia sells it, a year costs under €15. Optional third, given the muscle goal: creatine, 3 g a day — the only other supplement with real evidence behind it. Everything else in a supplement shop is a waste of money.

Water · 2 L

500 ml the moment you wake, before coffee. Part of the 11 a.m. tiredness is simply that you haven’t drunk anything since yesterday.

Coffee

Two a day, both before 14:00, never before food. On an empty stomach it causes the queasiness that makes breakfast impossible.

Sleep · 7.5 h

A 6:00 alarm means lights out at 22:30. Muscle is built and appetite hormones reset while you sleep. Phone across the room.

The 3 p.m. crash

A fuel problem, not your personality. Gone within ten days of eating breakfast, a 10:00 snack and a real lunch — your first proof this works.

The morning-after pill you took

Tiredness, nausea, headache and light spotting are all common and usually settle within a few days to a couple of weeks — so some of the exhaustion right now isn’t your diet. Two things follow: ignore the scale for two weeks (hormonal water easily adds 1–2 kg that isn’t fat), and expect your next period up to a week early or late. More than seven days late → take a pregnancy test. Start anyway; eating properly will help the fatigue.

Your cycle

  • The week before: appetite up, mood down. Keep the protein, eat the extra carbs without guilt, don’t weigh yourself. It passes in four days.
  • During: you lose iron — red meat or lentils, always with lemon (vitamin C roughly doubles absorption). Keep going to class unless you’re in real pain.
  • Irregular or absent periods mean long-term under-fuelling. Eat more, and see a doctor.

See a doctor if — not optional

  • The fatigue hasn’t clearly improved after four weeks of eating three real meals a day.
  • Dizziness standing up, hair falling out, cold all the time, nails breaking, bruising easily.
  • Periods that stop for three months or more.
  • Heart racing at rest, or breathless on stairs you used to manage.
  • Food starts to feel like anxiety rather than fuel — talk to someone early, it’s common and treatable.
  • Ask for this panel: ferritin, full blood count, vitamin D, B12, folate, TSH. Low ferritin is the most common cause of exactly this exhaustion, and it’s easy to fix.

Guidance for a healthy twenty-year-old with no known conditions — not medical advice. If a doctor says otherwise, the doctor wins.

One last page

Read this on the bad days

“I skipped a meal.”

Eat the next one normally. That’s the whole answer. Never eat less later to balance it — that’s the exact loop that created this.

“I ate way too much.”

Enjoy it, then back to normal at the next meal. One big meal cannot undo a week. Three days of under-eating afterwards absolutely can.

“The scale went up.”

Salt, carbs, your period, training yesterday, or your muscles refilling with water. None of it is fat. Weigh again in three days.

“I’m stuck at the same weight.”

Check three things honestly: three meals, 130 g protein, the steps. Nine plateaus out of ten are one of those — and the fix is doing them, not eating less.

“I feel guilty eating this much.”

At 59 kg and 1.60 m, 1,600–1,900 kcal is not overeating. It’s what a healthy adult woman needs. The guilt is a habit from the under-eating, and it fades.

“I fell off. It’s been a week.”
