Why you have no energy, aren’t losing fat,
and are losing muscle

All three of those complaints have the same single cause, and it isn’t the one you think. You are not eating too much. You are eating far too little, for far too long — and almost no protein at all.

What your body did about it

When food stays scarce for months, the body doesn’t “burn fat faster”. It protects itself. It turns the thermostat down: less heat, less spontaneous movement, slower digestion, foggier head, colder hands, flatter mood.

And because it still needs amino acids every single day to repair skin, hair, hormones and immune cells, and you aren’t eating any, it takes them from the only store you have — your muscle. That is the tissue you are losing.

Why the scale stopped moving

A body burning less and moving less needs less. Your maintenance drifted down until it met the tiny amount you were eating. That’s the stall.

Meanwhile muscle is the tissue that burns calories at rest. Every kilo you lose makes the problem worse. That’s why eating even less has stopped working — and why it will never work again.

The fix is not less food. It’s more — and better built.

The whole program in one sentence

Eat three real meals every day, put protein in every one of them, keep walking, go to Pilates three times a week, and sleep — and your body will let go of fat instead of muscle, because for the first time it can afford to.

Three things to accept before week 1

1
You will eat more than you have in a year.
Roughly 1,900 kcal a day for the first three weeks. That is not a lot — it’s normal. It only feels enormous because you’ve been running on half of it.
2
The scale may go up in weeks 1–3.
By 0.5–1.5 kg. It is water, glycogen and food in your gut — the three things that refill when you start eating. It is physically impossible for it to be fat at these calories. Do not panic and do not cut.
3
Nothing here is “clean eating”.
There is bread, rice, olive oil, merguez, dates and dark chocolate in this document. You already cook better than most people ever will. The only things missing were quantity and protein.

Don’t chase 55.0 on the scale

You are going to lose roughly 4 kg of fat and add 1–1.5 kg of muscle. If the scale stops at 56 kg, you will look like someone who lost six. Muscle takes up far less room than fat for the same weight — so the tape measure and your jeans will tell the truth long before the scale agrees. Track the waist.

Non-negotiables

The seven rules

If you forget everything else in this document, keep these. They do 90 % of the work.

1

Three meals. Every day. No exceptions.

Breakfast, lunch, dinner — even a small one, even late, even when you’re not hungry. Skipping a meal is what taught your body to slow down. Twenty-one meals a week is the habit that reverses it. A four-minute plate of tuna, chickpeas, olive oil and lemon counts as a meal. Nothing does not.

2

Protein first, at every single meal

Build each plate around the protein, then add the rest. Breakfast 30 g Lunch 40–45 g Dinner 30 g Snacks 15–20 g Protein is the only macro that signals your body to keep — and add — muscle while losing fat. It also keeps you full for hours, which is why you will stop crashing at 3 p.m.

3

Never go below 1,600 kcal

Not on a busy day, not on a bad day, not to “make up” for yesterday. Below that line you are back in the exact hole this program exists to get you out of. There is no version of this where eating less makes it faster.

4

Season everything properly

Harissa, garlic, cumin, sumac, lemon, olive oil, fresh parsley and mint. Flavour costs almost no calories and it is the entire reason this will still be running in week 12. Bland food is not healthier — it is just food you will stop eating.

5

2–2.5 litres of water a day

Half a litre the moment you wake up, before coffee. A bottle in your bag for class. A large part of the tiredness you feel at 11 a.m. is simply that you haven’t drunk anything since yesterday.

6

Lights out by 22:30

You wake at 6:00. That’s 7.5 hours. Muscle is built and appetite hormones are reset during sleep — short sleep raises hunger, lowers willpower and blunts every gain from the Pilates studio. Phone on the other side of the room.

7

Walk, every day, whatever else happens

6,000–10,000 steps. Steps are the quiet engine of this whole plan: they burn more over a week than any workout would, they cost you no recovery, and they don’t make you hungry the way cardio does. They happen on Pilates days too.

Weekends & days off

The structure stays, the clock moves. Wake later, but still eat three times — the two habits that break programs are “I woke up at 11 so I skipped breakfast” and “I’ll just eat once today”. Family meals are welcome: take a normal plate, add protein where you can, and skip the second round of bread.

Turning Phase 1 into Phase 2 — change three things, nothing else

From week 4 you drop about 200 kcal a day. You do not drop a meal, and you do not touch the protein or the olive oil. You shave the starch slightly, in three places:

−80 kcal

Lunch grain

160 g cooked rice / bulgur → 100 g. Roughly one heaped serving spoon less. Fill the gap on the plate with more salad.

−70 kcal

Breakfast starch

Two slices of bread → one, or 45 g of oats → 30 g. Same eggs, same yogurt, same nuts.

−60 kcal

Afternoon snack

Choose from the ≤ 150 kcal half of the snack list instead of the larger ones. You still snack. Every day.

Why Phase 1 exists at all

Three weeks of eating at maintenance before any deficit. It refills glycogen, restores your thyroid and appetite signalling, brings your energy back and gets the three-meal habit locked in. Cutting from a starved baseline is how people lose muscle and stall by week five. We are not doing that. In weeks 1–3, success is 21 days of three meals — not a number on the scale.

Two rules for reading the menus

  • Weights are the raw weight of meat and fish, and the cooked weight of grains, unless stated.
  • Anything marked ★ is a “worst day” option — fastest to make, hardest to talk yourself out of. Learn those four first and the rest is decoration.
06:15 · 30 g protein · 10 minutes

Breakfast

This is the meal that changes everything else. Eating 30 g of protein before 7 a.m. is what kills the 11 a.m. shakiness, the 3 p.m. collapse and the evening over-hunger — all three, within about ten days. Pick one, make it, eat it sitting down. Coffee after, not before.

B1

Express Shakshuka ★

10 min
You need
  • 3 eggs
  • 200 g tinned crushed tomato (half a tin)
  • ½ onion or ½ green pepper, sliced thin
  • 1 tsp harissa · ½ tsp cumin · salt
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • 1 small wholemeal pita (60 g)
Method

Oil in a small pan on high. Onion or pepper, 3 minutes. Tomato, harissa, cumin and salt, 3 minutes more until it thickens. Crack the eggs straight in, lid on, 4 minutes.

Eat it out of the pan with the pita. One pan to wash.

455kcal
27 gprotein
Sunday, cook the tomato base for four portions in one pot and keep it in a jar. That isn’t meal prep — it’s a sauce in the fridge. Breakfast then takes four minutes.
B2

Labneh, Za’atar & Egg Toast

6 min
You need

Method

Toast the bread. Spread the labneh thick, pour the oil over, rain za’atar on top. Peel the eggs, halve them, salt and black pepper.

Tomatoes and cucumber on the side. Done before the kettle boils.

470kcal
30 gprotein
Boil six eggs at once on Sunday and Wednesday evening. They keep a week in the fridge and they are the single most useful thing you can have in there.

Feta & Tomato Scramble Wrap

The 90-Second Emergency ★

“But I’m never hungry in the morning”

Expected, and temporary. Your stomach has adapted to being empty and your hunger hormones have gone quiet — that’s a symptom of the problem, not a preference. For the first five days use B6, which is drinkable and asks nothing of your appetite. Then move to B3, then to the cooked ones. Real morning hunger usually comes back within ten to fourteen days of eating regularly, and when it does, it’s one of the clearest signs that your metabolism is waking up.

13:15 · 40–45 g protein · the big meal

Lunch

You’re home, your appetite is highest, and you have the afternoon — so the biggest meal of the day goes here. Nothing takes more than 25 minutes, and most take under 20.

L1

Chicken Shawarma Bowl ★

20 min
You need
  • 160 g chicken thigh or breast, cut in strips
  • Marinade: 2 tbsp yogurt · ½ lemon · 1 clove garlic · 1 tsp baharat · salt
  • 160 g cooked rice or bulgur (100 g in Phase 2)
  • Tomato, cucumber, red onion, parsley, sumac
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp tahini + water + lemon + garlic
Method

Marinate in the bowl you’ll eat from while the rice cooks — ten minutes is plenty, it doesn’t need an hour. Then: pan as hot as you dare, no crowding, 8 minutes, don’t stir constantly. You want dark edges.

Rice down, chicken on top, salad beside, tahini loosened with cold water over everything.

640kcal
46 gprotein
The one to learn first. Change only the spice and it becomes four different meals — see the box on page 12.
L2

Ojja Merguez

15 min
You need
  • 2 halal merguez (100 g), sliced thick — or 120 g halal chicken sausage
  • 1 green pepper, sliced · 2 tomatoes or ½ tin crushed
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp harissa · 1 tsp tabil or cumin · 2 cloves garlic
  • 60 g bread, for mopping
Method

Merguez in a dry pan first — it gives up its own fat and you cook everything else in it. Three minutes, then pepper and garlic, three more. Tomato, harissa and tabil in, and let it reduce properly — 5 minutes, until it stops being watery.

Two wells, two eggs, lid on, 3–4 minutes. Yolks still soft. Bread.

620kcal
38 gprotein
Merguez is fatty, which is why the portion is 100 g and why there’s no added oil. It’s in the budget — enjoy it once or twice a week.
L3

Kefta & Bulgur

20 min
You need
  • 150 g beef mince 5 % fat
  • ½ onion grated · large handful parsley · 1 tsp cumin · ½ tsp paprika · salt, pepper
  • 150 g cooked bulgur or freekeh (100 g in Phase 2)
  • Cucumber yogurt: 120 g Greek yogurt, grated cucumber, dried mint, garlic
Method

Bulgur on — 12 minutes, no attention. Squeeze the water out of the grated onion first or the patties fall apart. Mix the mince with everything, shape four small flat patties, hot pan, 4 minutes a side.

Yogurt sauce stirred together while they cook. Everything on one plate.

630kcal
47 gprotein
Highest-iron meal in the book. Put it in the week your period is due — you lose iron then, and low iron is the most common cause of exactly the tiredness you’ve been describing.
L4

Lablabi Bowl

12 min
You need
  • 1 tin chickpeas (240 g drained)
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed · 1 tsp cumin · stock or water
  • 2 eggs, soft-boiled or poached
  • 1 tbsp harissa · 1 tbsp olive oil · juice of ½ lemon
  • 40 g day-old bread, torn
  • Parsley, capers, olives, a little raw onion
Method

Chickpeas into a small pan with just enough water to cover, the garlic and the cumin. Simmer 8 minutes until the garlic has gone soft and sweet.

Torn bread into the bottom of a deep bowl. Ladle the chickpeas and their broth over it.

Eggs on top, then harissa, olive oil, lemon, and everything green and salty you have.

610kcal
34 gprotein
Costs about €1.50 and takes twelve minutes. The meat-free day that doesn’t feel like a meat-free day.
L5

Salmon & Batata Harra

25 min (20 hands-off)
You need
  • 150 g salmon fillet
  • 250 g potatoes, cut in 2 cm cubes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil · smoked paprika · 3 cloves garlic · chilli · big handful coriander
  • Lemon · salt

Method

Oven 210 °C. Potatoes tossed with the oil, paprika and salt, on the tray, 20 minutes.

At 12 minutes, push them aside and put the salmon on the same tray, skin down, lemon and salt on it.

Out of the oven: garlic, chilli and coriander through the hot potatoes so they cook in the residual heat and stay sharp. Lemon over everything.

Harissa Chicken Pasta

L8

Halloumi & Lentil Plate

15 min
You need
  • 100 g halloumi (or queso para freír)
  • 200 g cooked green or brown lentils, tinned is fine
  • 1 tsp cumin · 2 cloves garlic · lemon · 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Tomato, onion and parsley salad

Method

Lentils rinsed, then warmed through with the garlic, cumin and olive oil — 5 minutes, and finish with a lot of lemon.

Lentils down, halloumi on, salad beside, pita to scoop.

Rotate the spice, not the recipe

The fastest way to never get bored: cook the same 150 g of chicken and change one thing. Three minutes of thought, a completely different meal, and no new shopping.

  • Baharat + yogurt + lemon → shawarma
  • Harissa + tomato + garlic → Tunisian
  • Sumac + onion + olive oil → musakhan
  • Ras el hanout + preserved lemon → tagine

The same applies to the grain — rice, bulgur, freekeh, couscous and potatoes are interchangeable at equal cooked weight. And to the protein: chicken, beef, lamb, fish, halloumi and chickpeas all slot into any of these plates.

20:15 · 30 g protein · 15 minutes or less

Dinner

Light, warm and small — but never skipped and never protein-free. Eating a real dinner is what lets you sleep properly and wake up able to eat breakfast. Three of the eight below need no cooking at all.

D1

Chorba Frik

20 min · makes 2
You need
  • 120 g lamb or chicken, cut very small
  • 40 g frik (green wheat) or orzo
  • 1 onion · 1 stick celery · 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • Tabil or cumin + coriander · a pinch of cinnamon
  • Parsley, coriander, and lemon at the end
Method

Meat and onion in the pot with a little oil until coloured. Tomato purée, spices, 1 minute — cooking the purée is what removes the tinny taste.

Water or stock, frik in, 15 minutes at a lazy simmer.

Herbs and a hard squeeze of lemon off the heat. The second portion is tomorrow’s dinner and it will be better.

420kcal
32 gprotein
The one exception to “no meal prep”: it makes two and improves overnight. Cook it Sunday and Wednesday and two dinners are solved.
D2

Shorbet Adas + Eggs

20 min
You need
  • 80 g red lentils
  • 1 onion · 1 tsp cumin · ½ tsp turmeric · 1 clove garlic
  • 600 ml stock
  • 2 eggs, boiled
  • Lemon · olive oil · toasted pita, broken
Method

Onion softened, spices in for a minute, lentils and stock, 15 minutes with the lid on.

Blend it smooth — a stick blender straight in the pot, no extra washing up.

Lemon, a thread of olive oil, pita chips on top. The two eggs on the side are what take it from 15 g of protein to 28.

430kcal
28 gprotein
Lentil soup alone is a carb meal, not a dinner. The eggs are the point — never serve it without them.
D3

Omelette Blanche & Big Salad

8 min
You need
  • 3 whole eggs + 1 extra white
  • 2 handfuls spinach · 25 g feta · dill and parsley
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • Tomato, cucumber and onion salad, lemon and olive oil
Method

Spinach wilted in the dry pan first, 1 minute, then tipped out — otherwise it waters down the eggs.

Oil, beaten eggs, low heat, lid on for 4 minutes. Spinach, feta and herbs over one half, fold.

The salad should be bigger than the omelette. That’s the ratio that makes a light dinner feel like a meal.

400kcal
31 gprotein
Eight minutes, one pan, and you always have the ingredients. This is the dinner for the night you get home late and annoyed.

Grilled Chicken Fattoush

D6

Baked White Fish & Loubia

18 min
You need
  • 160 g cod, hake or any white fish
  • 250 g green beans, fresh or frozen
  • 2 tomatoes or ½ tin · 3 cloves garlic · 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Cumin · coriander leaf · lemon
Method

Why dinner is the small meal

Not because eating at night makes you fat — it doesn’t. Because your appetite is largest at 13:00 when you get home, and because a lighter evening meal means you actually wake up hungry, which is what makes breakfast possible. The calories have to go somewhere; this is simply the arrangement that fits your day. Light is not the same as absent. 30 g of protein, every night.

Still hungry after dinner?

First, drink 300 ml of water and wait ten minutes — thirst reads as hunger far more often than people believe. Still hungry? Then eat: a yogurt, a piece of fruit, more salad, another egg. Real hunger after a 1,700 kcal day is information, not weakness. It usually means lunch was too small or you missed the afternoon snack. Fix that tomorrow rather than going to bed hungry tonight.

Pilates — how to make it build muscle

Pilates absolutely can build muscle, but only if it is treated as resistance training rather than as a stretching class. The difference is entirely in how hard the last few repetitions are. Here is how to get the most out of the three hours a week you have.

Shift the days to fit your timetable — what matters is three classes and at least one rest day between two of them, not which day they fall on.

Six rules for the studio

  • Reformer over mat, whenever you have the choice. Springs give you something to progress against; your own body weight doesn’t.
  • Ask for heavier springs. Tell the instructor your goal is strength, not “toning”. They will adjust the class for you — this is a normal request.
  • The last 2–3 reps should be genuinely hard. Legs shaking is not a problem, it is the entire point. If a set is comfortable all the way through, it built nothing.
  • Progress the load, not the reps. Doing thirty easy repetitions is cardio. Doing ten hard ones is training.
  • Slow the lowering phase. Three seconds down on everything. This is free extra stimulus and it costs no equipment.
  • Ankle weights or a band in mat classes, if the studio allows it. 1–2 kg changes everything on leg and glute work.

Never go to class fasted

Breakfast and the mid-morning snack are compulsory on class days. Training with an empty tank is exactly how you lose muscle instead of building it — the body has no fuel and no amino acids available, so it takes both from the tissue you are trying to keep. If class lands after 17:30, eat the afternoon snack first.

Protein around the class

Within roughly two hours either side — and the window is genuinely that wide, so don’t stress about it. In practice: lunch at 13:15 before a 16:30 class, and the yogurt or shake straight after. The class is the signal to build; the protein is the material. One without the other does nothing.

Supplements

You’ve never taken any, and you don’t need many. Two are genuinely useful here; a third depends on a blood test. Everything else in a supplement shop is a waste of money.

1 · Whey protein

The main tool

Why

Going from roughly 40 g of protein a day to 120 g is a big jump, and while your appetite is still small, food alone won’t get you there. One scoop is 24 g in thirty seconds, no cooking and no appetite required. It is powdered milk protein — that is all it is.

How

  • Dose: one scoop (~30 g) a day. Two on a heavy day.
  • When: whichever meal is lightest — usually the mid-morning snack, or breakfast on a rushed morning, or straight after Pilates.
  • Which: plain concentrate or isolate, vanilla or chocolate. Look for a halal mark and check the ingredients for gelatin — HSN, Prozis and MyProtein all list halal-certified lines and deliver across Spain in a day or two.
  • Cost: about €25–30 per kg — 33 servings, roughly €0.80 a day.
  • If it upsets your stomach: switch to isolate, which is far lower in lactose.

2 · Vitamin D3

1,000–2,000 IU daily

You leave at 07:20 and you’re indoors from 08:00 to 12:00 — most of the useful daylight, especially between October and March. Low vitamin D is very common in students and presents as exactly what you’ve been describing: tired, flat, slow to recover, low mood. Take it with a meal containing fat; breakfast with eggs is ideal. Any farmacia sells it over the counter and a year’s supply costs under €15.

3 · Iron — only if a blood test says so

Do not self-prescribe

Low ferritin is the most common reason a twenty-year-old woman feels exhausted, and it is very easy to fix — once it has been measured. Supplementing iron you don’t need is genuinely harmful, so this one needs a number, not a guess.

If the fatigue hasn’t lifted after four weeks of eating properly, ask a GP for ferritin, full blood count, vitamin D, B12 and TSH. Meanwhile the food does a lot of the work — L3, L8 and D2, always with lemon.

What to skip

Fat burners, detox teas, “slimming” infusions, CLA, L-carnitine, appetite suppressants, collagen for weight loss. None of them work, several aren’t halal-certified, and some sold online are genuinely unsafe. Creatine is the one other supplement with real evidence behind it, but it isn’t a priority for you now.

Caffeine

  • Never on an empty stomach. Coffee before food causes exactly the shaky, slightly nauseous feeling that then makes breakfast impossible. Eat first, coffee second — this one change alone will help.
  • Not a replacement for food. Coffee borrows energy; it doesn’t create it, and it has been covering for meals you weren’t eating.

Sleep · 7.5 hours

A 06:00 alarm means lights out at 22:30. Not negotiable, because almost everything this program is trying to do happens while you’re asleep: muscle repair, hormone reset, appetite regulation.

  • Screens down at 21:45
  • Phone charging across the room, not by the pillow
  • Room dark and cool
  • Last coffee before 14:00
  • The after-dinner walk makes falling asleep noticeably easier

Short sleep raises hunger hormones, lowers willpower and reduces how much muscle you build from the same class. It is not a small lever.

The 3 p.m. crash

It is not normal, it is not your personality, and it is not something to solve with more coffee. It is a blood sugar and fuel problem caused by eating too little too late in the day.

It should disappear within about ten days of eating breakfast, a mid-morning snack and a real lunch. That is one of the first and clearest signs the program is working.

If it is still there after four weeks of genuinely eating three meals a day — that’s the blood test on page 27. Don’t wait longer than that.

Honest, short, important

Health notes

The morning-after pill you took four days ago

Tiredness, nausea, headache, sore breasts and light spotting are all common, well-documented side effects, and they usually settle within a few days to a couple of weeks — so some of the exhaustion you’re feeling right now has nothing to do with your diet. Two practical consequences for this program:

  • Ignore the scale for the next ten to fourteen days. Hormonal water retention can easily add one to two kilos that has nothing whatsoever to do with fat. Weighing yourself during this window will only upset you over nothing.
  • Your next period may arrive early or late — up to about a week either way is expected. If it is more than seven days later than you’d expect, take a pregnancy test, because the pill reduces the chance of pregnancy but does not eliminate it.

Start the program now anyway. Eating properly will help the fatigue, not add to it.

Your cycle, and how to work with it

  • The week before your period: appetite up, water weight up, mood down. This is when programs get abandoned. The plan: keep protein high, eat the carbs without guilt, and do not weigh yourself. It passes in four days.
  • During your period: you lose iron. Prioritise L3 (kefta), L8 (lentils) and D2, always with lemon — vitamin C roughly doubles iron absorption. Keep going to Pilates unless you’re in real pain; gentle movement genuinely helps cramps.
  • If your periods are irregular, very light, or have stopped: that is a signal that your body has been under-fuelled for a long time. It is a reason to eat more, not less — and a reason to see a doctor rather than wait it out.

See a doctor if — not optional

  • The fatigue hasn’t clearly improved after four weeks of eating three real meals a day.
  • Dizziness when you stand up, hair falling out more than usual, feeling cold all the time, nails breaking, bruising easily.
  • Periods that stop for three months or more.
  • Heart racing at rest, or breathlessness on stairs you used to manage easily.
  • Food starts to feel like anxiety rather than fuel — talk to someone about that early, not late. It is far more common than people admit, and it is very treatable.

Week 16 is not the finish line

It’s the point where you stop losing and start maintaining — eating around 1,900 kcal, still with 120 g of protein, still at Pilates three times a week. Almost everyone who regains weight does it because they treated the end of the diet as the end of the habits. The habits are the result; the weight is just a side effect of them.

Read the weight column loosely

Those numbers are a trend line, not a schedule. Real weeks go: down 0.6, up 0.2, flat, down 0.5. Two flat weeks in a row is normal and means nothing. What matters is the direction across a month — and, far more than the scale, what the tape measure around your waist is doing.

Three ways this goes wrong — all avoidable

Skipping Phase 1
Going straight into a deficit from a starved baseline. You lose muscle, your energy never recovers, and you stall by week five.
Cutting harder when it slows
The instinct that got you here. Below 1,600 kcal the body defends itself and the whole thing grinds to a halt.
Quitting in the flat month
There will be a stretch where the scale barely moves while your waist drops. That’s muscle replacing fat. Keep going.

How to track progress

You are going to build muscle while losing fat. That means there will be a month where the scale barely moves and your waist drops three centimetres. If the scale is the only thing you measure, you will quit during exactly that month. Don’t let that happen.

Same day, same time, after the bathroom, before eating or drinking, same clothes or none.

  1. Weight. Write it down — but only the four-week trend means anything. A single reading is noise.
  2. Waist, at the navel, tape flat, don’t pull it tight, don’t hold your breath in. This is the number that matters most.
  3. Hips, at the widest point.
  4. Photos every two weeks — front and side, same spot, same light, same time of day. You will not notice change day to day. You will absolutely notice it between photo one and photo six.

Daily · ten seconds in a notes app

Five ticks. That’s the whole system.

  • Three meals? Yes / No
  • Protein target? Yes / No
  • Steps
  • Energy, out of 10
  • Hours slept

After four weeks this little log is more useful than any app, because when something stalls you can look back and see immediately which of the five slipped. It is nearly always one of the first two.

Do not weigh yourself every day

Daily weight swings by up to 1.5 kg from salt, water, carbs, hormones, hydration and how full your gut is — none of which is fat. Gaining a kilo of actual fat would require eating about 7,700 kcal above your maintenance. You did not do that overnight. Once a week, Saturday, and then put the scale away.

When it gets hard

Troubleshooting

Every one of these will happen at some point in sixteen weeks. None of them is a reason to stop.

“I’m never hungry in the morning.”

Completely expected, and temporary — your hunger signalling has gone quiet because you’ve trained it to. Don’t force a full plate. Use B6, the 90-second shake, for the first five days; liquid asks nothing of your appetite. Then move to B3, then to the cooked breakfasts. And stop drinking coffee before food — it is suppressing what little appetite you have. Real morning hunger usually returns within two weeks.

“I don’t have time to cook.”

Nothing in this document takes more than 25 minutes, and half of it takes under ten. D5 (5 min), D8 (4 min), L6 (10 min) and B3 (2 min) involve no cooking at all. On the very worst day: tuna, tinned chickpeas, olive oil, lemon, parsley, bread. Four minutes, 40 g of protein, and it is a complete meal — not a compromise.

“I skipped a meal.”

Then eat the next one normally. That is the entire answer. Do not eat less later to balance it out — that is the exact loop that created this problem. A missed meal is a missed meal, not a debt to be repaid.

“I ate way too much / there was a big family meal.”

Good. Enjoy it fully, and go back to normal at the very next meal — see Day C on page 17. One large meal cannot undo a week; it isn’t mathematically possible. Under-eating for three days afterwards to “fix” it absolutely can, because that’s three days of no protein, no energy and a signal to your body that food is scarce again.

“The scale went up.”

Run through the list: more salt than usual, more carbs than usual, period due, trained yesterday, constipated, drank less water, or — most likely in your first month — you’ve just started eating properly again and your muscles are refilling with glycogen and water. Every one of those adds weight that is not fat. Weigh again in three days and look at the trend, not the reading.

“I’m bored of the food.”

Rotate the spice, not the recipe — page 12. The same 150 g of chicken becomes shawarma, Tunisian, musakhan or tagine depending on one jar. Three minutes of thought, no new shopping, an entirely different meal. And swap the grain: rice, bulgur, freekeh, couscous and potatoes are interchangeable.

“Someone cooked for me and I can’t control the portions.”

Eat it, and enjoy it. Take a normal plate, go to the protein first, take one round of bread rather than three, and don’t take a second helping automatically. Tunisian and Syrian home cooking was never the problem here — the problem was eating almost nothing at all. Refusing food at your family’s table to protect a calorie target is the wrong trade, every time.

“Pilates was cancelled this week.”

Change nothing about the food. Add 2,000 steps on that day and carry on. One missed class across sixteen weeks is statistical noise. Two missed weeks in a row is worth solving — find a second studio or a class at a different time so a cancellation never costs you the whole week.

“I’ve been stuck at the same weight for three weeks.”

First, check honestly, with the daily log in front of you: are you actually eating three meals? Are you actually hitting 120 g of protein? Are you actually getting the steps? Nine plateaus out of ten are one of those three, and the answer is to fix it rather than to eat less.

If all three are genuinely yes for three straight weeks, and the waist hasn’t moved either, then reduce by about 100 kcal — one less tablespoon of oil, or a slightly smaller grain portion at lunch. That’s it. Never below 1,600.

“It’s the week before my period and I want to eat everything.”

Because your body genuinely needs a little more then — this is physiology, not a failure of discipline. Keep the protein high, let yourself have the extra carbs, add the dark chocolate, and do not weigh yourself. It lasts about four days and then it’s gone. Fighting it costs more than it saves.

“I feel guilty when I eat.”

Read this slowly: at 59 kg and 1.60 m, eating 1,700–1,900 kcal a day, you are not overeating. You are eating the amount a healthy adult woman needs. The guilt is a habit your body learned during a long stretch of under-eating, and it will fade as regular eating becomes normal again. If it doesn’t fade — or if it gets louder — talk to someone about it. That is a real thing, it is common, and it is treatable.

“I’m exhausted and I’ve been doing everything right.”

Give it four full weeks of three meals a day. If the fatigue is still there after that, stop troubleshooting the diet and go and get the blood test on page 26 — ferritin, full blood count, vitamin D, B12, TSH. Some tiredness is a nutrition problem. Some of it is a number that needs measuring, and no amount of eating well will fix it on its own.

Madrid, without the maths

Eating out

You are twenty and you live in Madrid. You are going to eat out, and that is entirely fine — it only needs a default order so you don’t have to think about it every time.

Shawarma & kebab

Lavapiés, Tetuán — and halal by default

Ask for a plato rather than a durum: meat, salad and a little rice instead of a huge wrap. Skip the chips and the soft drink. Extra garlic sauce is fine; it’s mostly nothing. Easily 45 g of protein. This is the best fast food in the city for you.

Poke bowls & salad bars

Poke House, Honest Greens

Double the protein — it’s usually €2–3 and it doubles the point of the meal. Half rice, half greens. Avocado yes; creamy sauce and crispy onions and mango together, no. Pick one indulgence, not three.

A Spanish menú del día

Starter: salad, gazpacho or vegetable soup. Main: anything a la plancha — pollo, merluza, atún, ternera. Dessert: fruit or yogurt. Avoid jamón, chorizo, morcilla, panceta and bacon, which rules out most of the bocadillo menu anyway.

Breakfast or coffee out

Tostada con tomate plus a portion of tortilla española and a café con leche is a genuinely decent 25 g breakfast. Ask for an extra egg if they’ll do it. Avoid the pastry case — a napolitana is 400 kcal and 5 g of protein.

Supermarket lunch in a hurry

Rotisserie chicken (pollo asado, check it’s halal or choose tinned tuna instead) + a bag of salad + a pot of hummus + a piece of fruit. About €6, 40 g of protein, no cooking, eaten on a bench. Better than most restaurant meals and a quarter of the price.

Coffee shops

Café con leche, not a frappuccino. A large flavoured or iced coffee can carry 350–450 kcal, almost all sugar, with two grams of protein — a meal’s worth of calories that will leave you hungry an hour later.

The only rule that matters when eating out

Find the protein, and don’t skip the meals around it. If lunch out was big, make dinner one of the light no-cook plates — D5 or D8. If lunch out was small and mostly bread, add a yogurt or a shake when you get home. Eating out is a normal part of a normal life; it only causes problems when it becomes a reason to skip everything else that day.

One last thing

Read this on the bad days

Panda — you are not lazy and your body is not broken.

It has been running on far too little for far too long, and it did the only sensible thing a body can do in that situation: it turned everything down, and it started taking apart the muscle you weren’t feeding. That is why you’re tired. That is why the scale stopped. That is why you’re losing exactly the tissue you wanted to keep. All three complaints, one cause — and the cause is not that you lack discipline. It’s that you haven’t been eating.

So look back through these pages and notice what isn’t in them. There is no forbidden list. There is bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, olive oil, merguez, halloumi, dates and dark chocolate. There is a whole page about spices, because food you don’t enjoy is food you’ll abandon by March. You already cook better than most people ever will. Nothing about how you cook needed fixing. The only things missing were quantity and protein.

The whole program, honestly, is four lines:

  • Eat three times a day.
  • Put protein in every one of them.
  • Walk, and go to your Pilates.
  • Sleep.

The other thirty-one pages are just the details — the menus so you never have to decide, the shopping list so you never run out, the troubleshooting so a bad day doesn’t become a bad month.

The first three weeks will be the hardest, and they will feel like nothing is happening. You’ll be eating more than you have in a year, the scale might go up, and every old instinct will tell you to cut back. Don’t. That is precisely the stretch where the work is being done underneath — glycogen refilling, hormones settling, appetite waking up.

By week six you’ll have your energy back, and you’ll notice it first in the smallest way: three o’clock will arrive and you just won’t crash.

By week sixteen you’ll have the body you actually wanted — built, not shrunk.
